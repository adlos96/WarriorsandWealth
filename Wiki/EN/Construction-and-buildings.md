# Construction and buildings

*The structures of the village, how they are built, what they really cost and how the queues work.*

---

## The village is built brick by brick

Your village grows by building **structures**: the ones that produce resources, the ones that produce equipment and the ones that house the troops. Structures aren't upgraded through levels: if you want more production or more space, you build **another one**. Each new structure adds to what the others already do, and the village grows by addition.

The place where everything is decided is the **Construction** tab in the bottom bar. At the top there are two buttons: **Buildings** and **Training** (the latter is explained in [Army](Army.md)). We stay on Buildings.

## The three families of structures

| Civil Structures | Military Structures | Barracks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Farm, Sawmill, Stone Quarry, Iron Mine, Gold Mine and Houses. They produce civilian resources and Population, and widen the storage of what they produce. They are the engine of the village. | The Sword, Spear, Bow, Shield, Armor and Arrow Workshops produce the equipment; the Trap Workshop builds the defenses. They are for those who want a real army. | Warrior, Spearman, Archer and Catapult Barracks. Without the right Barracks you can't train that class of troops, and each one houses a limited number. |

> [!NOTE]
> **What about the City defenses?**
>
> The Entrance, the Walls, the Gate, the Towers, the Center and the Castle are **not built here**: you find them in the **City** screen and they are strengthened through [Research](Research-and-Technology.md).

## How to build

1. Open **Construction → Buildings**. The structures are divided into the three groups: *Civil Structures*, *Military Structures* and *Barracks*.
2. For each structure use the **+** and **−** buttons to choose *how many* structures of that type you want. You can build several structures together.
3. Press the **i** button of a structure if you want to see what it costs, how long it takes and what it does: the cost you read is already multiplied by the quantity you chose.
4. Press **Build**. Everything you chose starts with a single submission, and the steppers go back to zero.

If you don't have enough resources or Population for everything you asked for, the game warns you and nothing starts. The cost is paid **immediately**, even for the structures that will have to wait in the queue.

## What a structure costs

Each structure requires **resources** and a small share of <img src="../../assets/mini/Popolazione_V2.png" width="20" height="20" align="absmiddle" alt=""> **Population**, and its **construction time** depends on the type. The price in resources, however, is not always the same: **the more structures of the same type you own (or already have in the queue), the more the next one costs**. The growth is gradual and proportional to the starting price of each structure (the more expensive ones always stay more expensive than the others), and the price never exceeds what your storage can sustain. The construction time, on the other hand, doesn't change with this mechanism.

This changes how you plan: building *everything of one type* in one go is never the cheapest way. A healthy village expands in steps, a bit of each family, following what it needs at that moment. You can always see the exact price of the next structure in the **i** box of the row.

## The queues: Builders

How many structures can be built *at the same time* is determined by your **Builders**, shown in the Overview screen, **Buildings** tab. You start with just one; more queues come with the Shop packs and with certain items. Anything beyond the available Builders waits its turn and starts on its own as soon as one becomes free.

In the Overview you see, for each structure, how many you have built and how many are queued; the *Under construction* bar and the **Time remaining** tell you where you stand.

- **Speed up.** It appears when something is under construction: every <img src="../../assets/mini/DiamanteBlu_V2.png" width="20" height="20" align="absmiddle" alt=""> **Blue Diamond** spent removes a bit of time, and the game shows you in advance how much.
- **Cancel queued buildings.** It cancels the structures that *haven't started yet* and refunds only **a part** of what you paid (the confirmation tells you how much). The ones already under construction can't be cancelled.

Times are also shortened by [Research](Research-and-Technology.md) (*Construction*), by [Technology](Research-and-Technology.md) and by the VIP bonus.

## Demolishing

In the Overview, at the bottom of each structure there is a trash-can button **Demolish**: you choose how many and the demolition is **instant**, but **it refunds nothing**. What you spent to build it is lost: think carefully before pressing.

> [!WARNING]
> **Two sensible limits**
>
> You can't demolish a **Barracks** if the troops of that class (trained, queued or deployed in defense) would no longer fit in the new limit: first you *dismiss* them or wait. The same goes for the **Trap Workshop** with the traps. The game always tells you how many you can demolish.

## The structures, one by one

### Civil structures

- **Farm, Sawmill, Stone Quarry, Iron Mine, Gold Mine.** One per resource: each produces its raw material and raises the storage limit of that resource. They have no upkeep costs: they just produce.
- **Houses.** They produce <img src="../../assets/mini/Popolazione_V2.png" width="20" height="20" align="absmiddle" alt=""> **Population**, which is needed to build, train and research. If Population runs out you can no longer build, train or research, even with full storages.

### Military structures

- **Sword, Spear, Bow, Shield, Armor, Arrow Workshops.** They produce the troops' equipment. The more Workshops of a type you own, the more that material grows and the more of it you can keep in storage. They have an **upkeep**: build only the ones needed by the units you really want to train.
- **Trap Workshop.** Without it you can't build traps. The number of Workshops decides how many traps you can own in total. It also has an upkeep.

### Barracks

- **Warrior, Spearman, Archer, Catapult Barracks.** They aren't upgraded individually: each Barracks adds places for that class. The number of troops you can have depends on how many Barracks you have: the detail is in [Army](Army.md).

## The strategist's advice

> [!TIP]
> **The order that works**
>
> At the start push the **basic resources** and the <img src="../../assets/mini/Abitazioni_V2.png" width="20" height="20" align="absmiddle" alt=""> **Houses**: without Population and without stocks you can't do anything else. When production holds, move on to the Workshops you need and only then to the Barracks. Building a Barracks before having the production to sustain it is the quickest way to get bogged down (if you want to understand why, read the paragraph on upkeep in [Resources](Resources.md)).

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary><b>Is there a maximum number of structures I can build?</b></summary>

The main brake is economic: the price rises with every structure of the same type and the upkeep weighs on production. For the precise numbers always look at the **i** box of the structure.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Why does my second Farm cost more than the first?</b></summary>

It's intended: every structure of the same type costs a bit more than the previous one. Plan your purchases in steps instead of making one big expense.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Does construction go on if I leave the game?</b></summary>

Yes: the ones in progress and the ones in the queue continue and finish even while you're offline.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>If I cancel a queued structure, do I get everything back?</b></summary>

No, only a part. If in doubt, check the confirmation message before pressing.

</details>
